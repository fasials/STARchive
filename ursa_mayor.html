Ursa Major

The Great Bear

INTRODUCTION

Recorded by : Ptolemy, 2nd century

Ursa Major, Latin for "the Great Bear", is one of the largest and best-known constellations in the northern sky. It was catalogued by the astronomer Ptolemy in the 2nd century and is among the 88 constellations recognised today. Its most famous part is the Big Dipper, a group of seven bright stars that forms the shape of a ladle. Because it circles the north celestial pole, it is visible all year from most places in the northern hemisphere. Its two outer "pointer" stars, Dubhe and Merak, point toward Polaris, which has helped travellers find north for centuries.
